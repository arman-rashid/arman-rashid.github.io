Research Focus

Instrumentation & methodology for molecular electronics

Development of advanced experimental methodologies to investigate charge transport across metal (or carbon) │ molecule (or SAM) │ metal (or carbon) junctions under applied electrical bias and temperature gradients. Research focuses on understanding how charge transport through single molecules and hybrid molecular assemblies is influenced by external stimuli, including light, magnetic fields, and chemical gating. Current research at Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT) centers on the development of advanced instrumentation for a liquid-helium-temperature scanning tunnelling microscope (STM) to investigate light–matter interactions and quantum transport at the nanoscale.

Technical Expertise & Research Skills

Designed, built, and optimized specialized instrumentation for molecular electronics research, including an EGaIn-based platform for molecular junction studies and scanning tunnelling microscope (STM) and mechanically controlled break junction (MCBJ) systems for nanoscale electrical measurements. Mechanical components were designed using Autodesk Inventor, with multiple iterations fabricated through 3D printing to achieve stable, high-performance experimental platforms.Developed custom LabVIEW software for instrument control, experimental automation, and data acquisition, together with data-processing algorithms for extracting meaningful correlations and key transport metrics from large, noisy datasets. These computational methods have contributed directly to peer-reviewed publications. In addition to extensive LabVIEW experience, proficient in Python for scientific programming, data analysis, and automation.Experienced with quantum-chemical calculations through master's research, primarily using Gaussian and, to a lesser extent, SIESTA. While computational transport calculations were not a primary focus of the PhD, the work provided a solid theoretical foundation that complements extensive experimental expertise. Research experience further includes UV–Vis absorption spectroscopy, fluorescence spectroscopy, atomic force microscopy (AFM), electrochemical characterization, and synthetic chemistry. An academic background spanning chemistry, biology, and biochemistry provides a broad interdisciplinary perspective for research across molecular electronics, materials science, and the life sciences.Combines expertise in instrument design, experimental physics, scientific software development, and interdisciplinary chemistry to develop advanced measurement platforms for nanoscale transport and molecular electronics.
