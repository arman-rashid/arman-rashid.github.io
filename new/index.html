01 — About

Chemist by training, instrument‑builder by obsession.

Below one nanometre, wires stop being wires. They become molecules — and molecules have opinions about how electrons should flow.

After a gold-medal Master’s in Chemistry at the University of Kashmir, I moved to Bangalore for a PhD at the Indian Institute of Science. There I worked on molecular electronics: taking a multidisciplinary approach to ask how single molecules — or ensembles of them — can mimic the functions of conventional electronic components, pushing miniaturisation past the limits where Moore’s law runs into quantum mechanics.

Today I’m a research fellow at the Physics Institute (PHI) and the Institute of Quantum Materials and Technologies (IQMT) at KIT, building instrumentation and studying light–matter interaction with a liquid-helium-temperature scanning tunnelling microscope — a magnificent beast, as I like to call it.

02 — Research

Quantum transport, one molecule at a time.

The field still lacks a consensus on how a molecule’s structure and conformation shape the charge it carries. My work is about building the tools — and the data — to settle that.

I develop instrumentation to probe electronic, thermal and thermoelectric properties of metal│molecule│metal and metal│SAM│metal junctions, often with a nanoscale electrochemical twist. By measuring across levels of complexity — from a single molecule to a self-assembled monolayer — we can pin down structure–property relationships and translate them into real devices for organic electronics, sensing, optoelectronics and energy harvesting.

Instrumentation

3 rigs · EGaIn · MCBJ · STM-BJ
Rig 01pA · ensemble

EGaIn platform

Large-area junctions measuring picoamp currents under bias (conductance) or a temperature gradient (thermopower) between a liquid-metal top contact and template-stripped Au / Ag.

Rig 02fA · single molecule

MCBJ

Mechanically controlled break junction resolving femtoamp currents across single metal│molecule│metal junctions — built-in piezo, bipotentiostat, and an open design for external stimuli.

Rig 03vertical · heated

STM-BJ

Vertical single-molecule junctions between a sharp tip and a flat electrode. A heated substrate opens up thermopower and spin-dependent transport.

03 — Try it

Break a junction. Thousands of times.

A break-junction experiment pulls two gold electrodes apart while measuring conductance. The gold neck thins to a single atom (1 G0), snaps — and if a molecule bridges the gap, you see a plateau far below.

Any single trace is noise and luck. Thousands of them, stacked into a histogram, reveal the molecule’s fingerprint. This is a toy simulation of exactly that.

Live trace. log(G/G0) as the electrodes separate.
Histogram. Every point from every trace. Watch the molecular peak grow.
Ghosts. The last dozen traces — no two junctions are alike.
2D histogram. Every trace aligned at the moment the gold contact breaks, binned by conductance and displacement — the molecular plateau shows up as a bright band, and its length tells you how far the molecule stretches.
